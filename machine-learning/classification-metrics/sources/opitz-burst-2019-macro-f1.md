# Opitz and Burst, "Macro F1 and Macro F1" (arXiv:1911.03347v3, a short note; v1 8 Nov 2019, v3 8 Feb 2021)

Supports: two formulas are used under the name "macro F1": "averaged F1", the arithmetic mean over per-class F1 (eq. 2), and "F1 of averages", the harmonic mean of the arithmetic means of precision and recall (eq. 3); in the second, each class's precision is multiplied with the recall of all other classes (Section 1); their confusion matrix is $m_{ij} = \lvert\{s \in S : f(s_1) = i \wedge s_2 = j\}\rvert$, rows predicted and columns true, with $n$ classes (Preliminaries); Theorem: F1 of averages ≥ averaged F1, strictly exactly when some class has $P_i \ne R_i$, equivalently when some $P_i < R_i$ and some $P_j > R_j$, and the supremum of the difference is 0.5 for even $n$ and $0.5 - 1/(2n^2)$ for odd $n$ (Section 2); the difference is large for classifiers with a strong bias toward certain error types, which the F1 of averages "rewards", and it is insensitive to which classes have which error distribution (Section 2); the two can rank classifiers differently, Tables 1 and 2 (Section 3); recommendation to use the averaged F1, "significantly more robust towards the error type distribution", and "at the very least, researchers should indicate which formula they are using" (Section 4); footnote 2 lists Sokolova and Lapalme (2009) among papers using the F1 of averages. The PDF text extraction lost the symbols that distinguish the two F1s; they were resolved from the arXiv LaTeX source (<https://arxiv.org/e-print/1911.03347>), where $\mathcal{F}_1$ is the averaged F1 and $\mathbb{F}_1$ the F1 of averages, so the Theorem's $\mathbb{F}_1 \ge \mathcal{F}_1$, the 'rewards' and 'insensitive' sentences and the Table 1 and 2 discussion all refer to the F1 of averages as the larger, benevolent one. Recomputing Tables 1 and 2 agrees.
