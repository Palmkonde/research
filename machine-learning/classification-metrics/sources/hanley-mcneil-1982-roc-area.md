# Hanley and McNeil, "The Meaning and Use of the Area under a Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) Curve" (Radiology 143(1): 29–36, 1982)

Supports: the ROC area is the probability that a randomly chosen diseased subject is rated or ranked with greater suspicion than a randomly chosen non-diseased subject, the same quantity the Wilcoxon statistic estimates (abstract, Section I); $W$ averages a score $S(x_A, x_N)$ over all $n_A \cdot n_N$ pairs, with $S = 1$ if $x_A > x_N$, $1/2$ if equal and $0$ if $x_A < x_N$, and depends only on rankings (Section III); on their CT example $W = 0.893$ "agrees exactly with the area under the ROC curve calculated by the trapezoidal rule" (Section IV).
