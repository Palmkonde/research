# McDermott, Zhang, Hansen, Angelotti and Gallifant, "A Closer Look at AUROC and AUPRC under Class Imbalance" (NeurIPS 2024; arXiv:2401.06091v4, 2025)

Supports: the paper refutes "Claim 1", that for a task with many more negatives than positives AUPRC gives a "better", "more accurate" or "fairer" evaluation than AUROC (abstract, Section 1); Theorem 1, $\text{AUROC}(f) = 1 - \mathbb{E}_{t \sim f(x) \mid y=1}[\text{FPR}(f,t)]$ and $\text{AUPRC}(f) = 1 - P(y=0)\,\mathbb{E}_{t \sim f(x) \mid y=1}[\text{FPR}(f,t)/P_x(f(x)>t)]$, with the Bayes-rule step $\text{Prec}(f,\tau) = 1 - P(y=0)\,\text{FPR}(f,\tau)/P_x(f(x)>\tau)$; apart from the fixed prevalence, AUROC weighs all false positives equally while AUPRC weighs them by the inverse of the model's firing rate (Section 2.1); Theorem 2, fixing any one misordered adjacent pair raises AUROC by the same amount and raises AUPRC more at higher scores, so the deciding factor is the use case, not class imbalance (Sections 1, 2.2); Theorem 3, for a model calibrated on the low-prevalence group, as that group's prevalence goes to 0 the AUPRC-maximizing fix lies in the higher-prevalence group (Section 2.3); synthetic experiments where AUPRC optimization favors the higher-prevalence group and AUROC treats groups about equally, and real fairness datasets with a milder effect that grows with the prevalence gap (Section 3); guidance to use AUROC for context-independent comparison, high false-negative cost and fair resource allocation, and AUPRC for high-cost, single-group false-positive reduction or information retrieval, while still reporting both metrics and curves (Section 4); 424 papers make Claim 1, 167 without citation, and the most-cited source, Davis and Goadrich, argues the PR curve may be more informative under imbalance but never that its area should be preferred; curves are communication tools, areas are summaries for deployment-agnostic comparison (Section 5); Table 3 marks "PR curves may better reflect deployment objectives" (citing Saito and Rehmsmeier among others) as valid, and calls "AUPRC depends on prevalence, which is desirable" too vague to evaluate (Appendix I).
