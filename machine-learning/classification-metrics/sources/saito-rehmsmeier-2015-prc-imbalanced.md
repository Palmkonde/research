# Saito and Rehmsmeier, "The Precision-Recall Plot Is More Informative than the ROC Plot When Evaluating Binary Classifiers on Imbalanced Datasets" (PLOS ONE 10(3): e0118432, 2015)

Supports: random classifiers lie on the ROC diagonal from (0, 0) to (1, 1), the ROC baseline, with AUC 0.5 for random and 1.0 for perfect classifiers; the PRC baseline is $y = P/(P+N)$ and moves with the class distribution, so the AUC (PRC) of a random classifier is $P/(P+N)$; PRC and ROC curves have a one-to-one relationship, but ROC uses linear and PRC non-linear interpolation; precision is "the fraction of correct predictions among the positive predictions"; the baseline position shows how hard the task is; "Only PRC changes with the ratio of positives and negatives" among the plots compared (Conclusion) Also (for the class-imbalance Finding): the abstract's claims that the visual interpretability of ROC plots on imbalanced data "can be deceptive" owing to "an intuitive but wrong interpretation of specificity", and that PRC plots can accurately predict future classification performance because they evaluate the fraction of true positives among positive predictions; Table 2, where ACC, sensitivity and specificity stay 0.6 on balanced and imbalanced data while precision goes 0.6 to 0.33 and MCC 0.2 to 0.17, only precision, MCC and the three $F_\beta$ scores vary, and precision reveals differences that accuracy leaves unnoticed; the simulation where ROC plots and AUC (ROC) are unchanged between balanced and imbalanced data, one ER- point means 160 FPs and 500 TPs balanced but 1600 FPs and 500 TPs imbalanced, ER+ and ER- share AUC (ROC) 0.8 although ER+ is better in early retrieval, so ROC needs special caution and an early-retrieval check on imbalanced data; the conclusion "strongly recommend PRC plots as the most informative visual analysis tool".
